### Clean transcription / topic map

The Preface introduces these foundational ideas:

- Living is an **art, skill, and technique**.
- Life is a **stream of experiences**.
- Every experience involves the **experiencer**, the **experienced object or situation**, and the relationship between them.
- Our happiness, peace, or disturbance often becomes dependent on the outcomes life gives us.
- Human beings often appear to fall into two tendencies: **active / productive / prosperous** and **peaceful / happy**.
- Vedanta seeks to unite **dynamic action with inner peace**.
- Knowledge of the unknown is approached through what is already known.
- Practical Vedanta studies the human personality, waking/dream/deep sleep, mind and intellect, and four paths of development.
- The seeker progresses through **dispassion**, **meditation**, and finally **Self-realization**.

## AI Explanation

The Preface works like a map of the entire study. It begins with the practical problem of living: how can a person remain active, productive, responsible, and successful without losing inner peace?

Vedanta does not begin by asking us to withdraw from life. It asks us to understand the person who is living. That is why the study moves from ordinary experience to the human personality, then to mind and intellect, then to deeper inquiry into the Self.

A useful summary is:

```text
Life = stream of experiences
        ↓
Examine the experiencer
        ↓
Understand body, mind, intellect
        ↓
Develop the personality through suitable paths
        ↓
Reduce dependence on external outcomes
        ↓
Dispassion and meditation
        ↓
Self-realization
```

The pages under this Preface section expand each of these topics separately.
