### Clean transcription

The Preface presents a step-by-step progression:

**Seeker → Dispassionate State → Meditation State → Self-Realized State**

This describes the gradual preparation of the personality for deeper realization.

## AI Explanation

### Seeker

The seeker begins with inquiry. Ordinary achievements and pleasures may still matter, but deeper questions begin to arise: Is lasting happiness possible through changing circumstances alone? Who am I beyond roles and possessions? Why do desire and dissatisfaction keep returning?

The seeker does not have to reject worldly life. The change is that ordinary assumptions begin to be examined.

### Dispassionate State

**Dispassion does not mean becoming emotionless, uncaring, or inactive.**

It means reducing psychological dependence on objects, people, circumstances, and outcomes for one's inner completeness.

A person can still love, work, enjoy, create, and participate fully in life. The difference is that the world is no longer expected to provide permanent inner security.

### Meditation State

As outward pull and dependence reduce, the mind becomes more capable of sustained contemplation.

Meditation here is more than relaxation. In the Vedantic journey, it becomes meaningful after the personality has gained sufficient preparation, discrimination, and relative freedom from overpowering attachment.

### Self-Realized State

Self-realization is presented as the culmination of the inquiry: recognition of one's essential nature rather than identification only with changing body, mind, roles, circumstances, and experiences.

```text
SEEKER
   ↓
DISPASSION
Reduced psychological dependence
   ↓
MEDITATION
Sustained contemplation
   ↓
SELF-REALIZATION
Recognition of the essential Self
```

### Practical reflection

When a strong desire or fear arises, ask:

**What am I expecting this outcome to give me internally?**

Security? Recognition? Importance? Peace? Completeness?

That question begins shifting attention from the external object toward the inner dependence behind it.

## Additional Notes & References

The word **dispassion** is often expressed in Vedantic literature through the idea of *vairagya*: freedom from excessive attachment or dependence. The precise depth and usage can vary by text and teacher, so this is provided as supplementary terminology rather than as a direct transcription of the class notes.

The progression on this page should be understood as a direction of inner development rather than as four rigid compartments that never overlap.
