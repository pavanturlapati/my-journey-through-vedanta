# Culmination of Knowledge

## Vedanta and the Real Self

<div class="handwritten-note">
Vedanta literally means <strong>culmination of knowledge</strong>. <em>Veda</em> means knowledge and <em>anta</em> means end. It is the knowledge of your real Self. The Self within is supreme, presently obscured by your body, mind and intellect. Vedanta helps you unfold your Self. Your real Being is divine.
</div>

<div class="ai-note">
The opening idea shifts Vedanta from collecting information to discovering the nature of the one who seeks knowledge. Body, mind and intellect are instruments through which we function, but Vedanta asks whether they are our complete identity.

The Self is described as divine. Without the knowledge to recognize that divinity, we identify ourselves primarily with body, mind and intellect. Vedanta therefore aims to remove this mistaken identification through knowledge; the deeper nature of the Self is discovered rather than created.
</div>

## The Intrinsic Wealth Within

<div class="handwritten-note">
Humanity is unaware of the intrinsic wealth within oneself. We are seeking wealth and enjoyment in the external world. We are governed universally by two motivations: <strong>(1) to acquire wealth and (2) to enjoy what is acquired.</strong>
</div>

<div class="ai-note">
Human effort is commonly directed outward: acquire something and then enjoy it. Vedanta does not reject prosperity or enjoyment, but questions the assumption that lasting fulfillment exists only outside us. If peace and completeness are intrinsic to the Self, endless external acquisition cannot by itself solve an internal problem of ignorance.
</div>
