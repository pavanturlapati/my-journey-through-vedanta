# Life as a Stream of Experiences

## Anubhava Dhara

<div class="handwritten-note">
Life is defined in Sanskrit as <em>anubhava dhara</em>, a <strong>stream of experiences</strong>. As long as experiences flow there is life. An experience is the unit of life. It is constituted of two factors: <strong>subject and object</strong>. The individual — you — is the subject and the world the object. When you contact the world, you gain an experience. The flow of experiences is life.
</div>

<div class="ai-note">
Vedanta analyzes life through its practical unit: experience. Every experience involves a subject, the individual who experiences, and an object, whatever is experienced. This becomes important because improving life requires examining both sides — not only the world we contact, but also the individual who contacts it.
</div>

## Science Improved the Object

<div class="handwritten-note">
Scientists worked on the object to provide humanity with greater peace and happiness. Science brought push-button comforts, but still humans are not as happy as they should be.
</div>

<div class="ai-note">
Science and technology have transformed the objective side of life. The Vedantic observation is not a criticism of science, but a question of scope. If every experience contains both subject and object, improving only the object cannot guarantee happiness. External comfort does not automatically remove anxiety, fear, attachment or dissatisfaction in the experiencer.
</div>

## The Sages Studied the Individual

<div class="handwritten-note">
Another class of thinkers called <strong>sages</strong> analyzed the anatomy of the experience and discovered that the individual was unattended, neglected to a point of fault. The sages thus gave out the <strong>sublime philosophy of Vedanta</strong>.
</div>

<div class="ai-note">
The sages turned attention toward the neglected half of experience: the subject. The knowledge is called <strong>sublime</strong> here because it aims toward excellence in life and recognition of the divinity of the Self. The Self is not made divine by Vedanta; knowledge helps remove the ignorance through which we identify ourselves only with body, mind and intellect.
</div>
